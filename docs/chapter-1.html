Chapter 1: Antibiotics

Antibiotics are a type of medicine used to treat infections caused by bacteria. They do this by stopping bacterial cells from building their cell wall, DNA/RNA, or proteins. Without one of those things, the cells eventually cannot survive and die off.

See the button below the image to view the affected areas on an example bacterial cell.

The cell wall surrounds the bacterial cell, protecting it from environmental conditions and helping maintain its structure. DNA or RNA is found as free-floating strands in the nucleolus, located in the center of the cell. Lastly, the proteins of a bacterial cell, crucial to survival and development, are mostly found in its ribosomes, represented by the small brown dots in the image.

Antibiotics that affect the cell wall are called bactericidal, and antibiotics that affect reproduction, so DNA, RNA, or proteins, are called bacteriostatic.

Some common types of antibiotics include:

  • Penicillins
  • Macrolides
  • Cephalosporins
  • Fluoroquinolones
  • Tetracyclines
Together, these drugs treat infections ranging from strep throat to acne to respiratory, skin, and ear infections, and so much more. They are essential to the world of medicine we know, and it is important for them to be taken only when necessary, keeping them effective.
