Chapter 1: Antibiotics

The cell wall, DNA/RNA, and proteins.

Antibiotics are a type of medicine used to treat infections caused by bacteria. They do this by stopping bacterial cells from building their cell wall, DNA/RNA, or proteins. Without one of those things, the cells eventually cannot survive and die off.
See the button below the image to view the affected areas on an example bacterial cell. The cell wall surrounds the bacterial cell, protecting it from environmental conditions and helping maintain its structure. The DNA or RNA is found in the form of free-floating strands in the center of the cell. Lastly, the proteins of a bacterial cell, crucial to survival and development, are mostly found in its ribosomes, represented by the small brown dots in the image.
